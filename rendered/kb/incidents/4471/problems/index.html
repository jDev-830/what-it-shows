#4471 · three problems, not one

owner Julian Villarreal open reviewed 2026-10-29 · 0 days ago

#4471 was merged as one issue and closed on 5 October when the parser fix shipped. Read the reports and the tenant they came from, and #4471 holds three problems. Closing it ended one, retired the form behind another, and left the third with no ticket.

The one-day moves are still open. Neither the parser fix nor retiring booking-v3 touches them.

5 of the parser reports are the one-day moves: the desk files them under parser by tenant, and they are counted again under shift.

How they differ

  • parser: the shared parser read every date month first. A tenant that writes the day first got day and month swapped: a 7 April reminder went out on 4 July. Every channel, every day-first tenant. Fixed on 5 October (fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales).
  • hint: booking-v3 printed MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, for tenants that write the day first. Their people typed what the hint said, and the form read it the tenant's way. It looked like the parser bug, but came only through the booking form of booking-v3 tenants, and the parser fix did not touch it. Won't fix: booking-v3 is retired.
  • shift: the date moves by one day. 12 May shows as 11 May; a client in Lisbon gets Tuesday while one in Toronto gets Monday; an invite arrives before the booking. No swap of day and month does that. It looks like a time-zone conversion. The desk had already noted these reports as one-day moves (desk-notes.json) and still filed them by tenant, under the parser, so they closed with it. The last one came in the day before the fix, from a reporter who has stopped reporting them: expect more than the table shows.

Why they were merged

Decision 0001 merges on the subject line alone. Subjects like "Wrong day on the invite" and "Date is wrong" come in for all three problems, so all three landed in #4471. The intake tag did not help: it is guessed from the reporter's browser, not from the tenant, and it disagrees with the tenant's cause on dozens of these reports.

Lessons

  • A big ticket can be several bugs that read the same. Before you mark it known, sort a sample by tenant and channel, and read the bodies.
  • Closing a merged ticket closes every report in it. Check each problem has its own fix first.
  • The export comes in overlapping pages: count unique report ids, not rows.
